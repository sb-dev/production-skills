# Extension Pack Bootstrap Process

**Status:** Canonical process  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 10 September 2026

## 1. Purpose and ownership

Create or revise evidence-qualified Extension Packs through two levels of work: curate complementary catalogue coverage, then research and prove each selected specialisation. Use this process during a new project bootstrap or independently in an established domain repository. Do not restart repository creation, redefine the entire discipline or regenerate all six specifications merely to add a pack.

Each pack has **exactly five justified foundational books**, but the books need not be unique to that pack. Reuse relevant examined evidence; do not require five newly acquired books or copy the core project's bibliography without a contribution assessment. Five books does not imply five packs or limit wider research.

Ownership remains:

| Surface | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| [Domain research process](domain-research-process.md) | Detailed Seed → Five → Challenge rules, permissions, source access, extraction and publication safeguards |
| This process | Catalogue curation, pack-specific application of research, execution gates and evidence reuse |
| [Family pack contract](../specs/03-production-skills-evaluation-and-extension-packs.md) | Qualification, precedence, evaluation and independent domain ownership |
| Domain specification 05 | Domain-native pack semantics, packaging and authoring contract |
| Domain specification 06 | Actual curated catalogue, source contributions, showcases and evidence status |
| Domain pack-authoring skill | Implement, demonstrate, evaluate and maintain self-contained packs |

Extend the domain's existing authoring capability rather than introducing a competing authoring system, shared pack runtime or central domain-knowledge store. Research logs are development evidence; installed packs must not require the central repository at runtime.

## 2. Entry conditions and execution rules

Read the owning domain's charter, relevant specifications, core-skill contracts, catalogue, existing packs, research logs and evaluation evidence at identified revisions. Establish the requested scope: catalogue design, one new pack, revision of an existing pack, implementation, or evaluation only.

For a new domain, a specified core can support pack research and design. Record that baseline as specified rather than measured. Production demonstration and differential evaluation require the relevant core implementation; do not fabricate a baseline or call a design-only comparison a production result.

Treat each stage below as a standalone task. Read its inputs, perform its substantive work, check its exit criteria and commit the detailed research log and completion evidence before dependent work proceeds. Keep per-pack selection, extraction and challenge separate. Shared research may be referenced by several packs, but each pack needs its own applicability decision and completion evidence.

Direct entry into a later stage is allowed only after reviewing the persisted evidence for its prerequisites. A narrow revision should revisit affected findings and tests, not rerun adequate research solely to preserve a sequence. Pending required approval blocks selection; inadequate source access blocks extraction. Record blockers without declaring the dependent stages complete.

Generating a catalogue or pack bootstrap specification defines intended work, not proof that its stages have run. Planned entries are permitted when clearly labelled; publication as a ready-to-use pack requires implementation and evaluation evidence.

## 3. Catalogue curation

**Inputs:** domain boundary, core capability model, existing catalogue and pack evidence, recurring production needs and explicit user constraints.

Map useful specialisation dimensions from the discipline: possible dimensions include medium, production format, audience, operational style, platform, performance, specialist workflow and evaluation needs. Do not impose narrative categories on other domains or enumerate every combination for symmetry.

Research or generate a broader candidate pool and compare candidates against a capability-coverage matrix. Assess combined coverage, practical reuse, distinct production behaviour, depth, evaluation feasibility and overlap with the core and neighbouring packs. Catalogue size follows useful complementary coverage, not the book count or an inherited catalogue size.

Classify each need:

```text
existing pack covers the need → reuse
one-project detail → project instructions
broadly applicable domain responsibility → core-improvement candidate
reusable specialised behaviour → create or refine a pack
insufficient value or evidence → defer or reject
```

Record why each selected pack belongs, what it adds, what overlaps intentionally and which gaps remain. Two packs sharing a medium may differ materially; different names or media do not themselves prove complementary behaviour.

For an existing catalogue, assess entries for retention, refinement, merging or retirement. Preserve approved identities, examples, installed interfaces and evidence until an explicit migration decision authorises a change. Catalogue curation does not authorise removing user-provided books or rewriting approved productions.

**Output:** candidate pool, coverage matrix, selection rationale, existing-entry dispositions, migration needs and per-pack research tasks. A single-pack request may perform this assessment against only the relevant part of the catalogue; it does not need a wholesale catalogue redesign.

**Exit:** the selected specialisations have a justified combined contribution, reuse/core alternatives are assessed, and planned work is distinguishable from completed evidence.

## 4. Per-pack bootstrap stages

### P1 — Define the specialisation and core baseline

**Inputs:** catalogue decision, production need, core contracts and any existing pack.

Define intended use and non-use, reusable outcomes, relevant core skills, adjacent boundaries, hard deliverable constraints, soft defaults and the expected difference from core behaviour. Identify what must remain stable. Explain why an existing pack plus ordinary project instructions is insufficient.

The baseline must include the actual production need, not an intentionally vague prompt that makes the pack appear useful. Record its core revision and whether it is specified or implemented.

**Output:** specialisation brief, baseline, expected behavioural difference, reuse/refinement decision and provisional evaluation questions.

**Exit:** a bounded reusable specialisation is justified without duplicating the core or taking over another domain.

### P2 — Select five complementary foundational books

**Inputs:** specialisation brief, supplied titles/files, existing domain and pack research.

Apply the selection, permission and access rules in [the domain research process](domain-research-process.md) to this pack's knowledge-coverage map. Select exactly five distinct books; editions of one work do not count as separate books. Research a broader candidate pool and justify each contribution to the pack, including relevant depth or contrast rather than unnecessary duplication.

Sources may be reused from the core domain or other packs. There is no quota of new books. Do not automatically inherit the core corpus or treat shared citations as completed specialist analysis. Clarify which supplied books are inputs to this pack; their removal, replacement or demotion requires explicit permission. Empty slots may be filled without an extra approval request unless separately required. Preserve declined and pending decisions, including the greater-than-five case, under the existing research contract.

Record identity, edition, intended contribution, provided/selected origin, source access, examined material and limitations separately. A book can be provisionally selected while access is obtained, but inaccessible books, summaries or model memory cannot satisfy extraction.

**Output:** pack coverage map, candidate comparison, five-book corpus, access register, source-reuse decisions and any substitution approvals.

**Exit:** exactly five books are selected, required permissions are resolved, and remaining access needs and coverage gaps are explicit.

### P3 — Extract and reconcile specialised knowledge

**Inputs:** completed corpus selection, accessible source material and reusable examined findings.

Meaningfully examine all five books for their intended contributions. For reused findings, inspect the original extraction, source locations, reading scope, assumptions, edition and evidential limits. Accept reuse only where that evidence supports the pack's intended use; examine missing or differently applicable material directly. A previous citation or another pack's profile is not proof of reading.

Map material findings as:

```text
source finding and location
→ applicability to this specialisation
→ change to a core-skill decision
→ observable artefact or workflow effect
→ evaluation criterion
→ failure and repair case
```

Reconcile overlap and disagreement without averaging incompatible methods or building one skill per book. Keep general domain knowledge, specialised guidance and project-specific detail distinct. A selected book may contribute little after examination; record that honestly rather than inventing a rule.

**Output:** per-book findings or reviewed reuse references, source-to-behaviour matrix, overlap/conflict analysis and provisional specialised guidance.

**Exit:** all five have been meaningfully examined for their contribution through direct analysis or adequate reviewed direct-source evidence; material guidance is traceable and limitations are explicit.

### P4 — Challenge claims and extend coverage

**Inputs:** original pack coverage map, extracted guidance, conflicts and unresolved questions.

Apply independent challenge and gap research. Seek supporting and contrary evidence, alternative methods, failure conditions and requirements absent from the books. Check change-sensitive practices against current authoritative sources. A generic domain research log is not sufficient unless it addresses this specialisation's questions.

Sources can include additional books, primary research, standards, practitioner material, official documentation and relevant productions. Films, games, recordings and other non-book works may supplement research but do not count towards the five books. Choose evidence appropriate to the claim; a creative convention is not an empirically established universal rule merely because it appears in a successful work.

Record disposition separately from evidential standing: retain, merge, adapt, qualify, reject or investigate further; supported finding, context-dependent method, practical heuristic, disputed claim or unresolved question. Retain useful uncertainty without silently making it an unconditional pack constraint.

**Output:** challenge and gap research, source independence/applicability assessment, claim dispositions and evidence-qualified specialised guidance.

**Exit:** material guidance has been assessed, important gaps addressed or bounded, and unsupported certainty removed.

### P5 — Specify behaviour and evaluation

**Inputs:** qualified guidance, baseline and domain pack contract.

Define dimensions, activation, constraints/defaults, relevant core-skill effects, artefact requirements, continuity where relevant, evaluation adjustments, smallest-sufficient repair, conflicts and handoffs. Explicit project instructions and approved work outrank pack defaults; surface conflicts rather than silently changing stronger decisions. The pack remains optional and the core remains useful without it.

Define falsifiable acceptance cases before implementing the pack or running its showcase. State what should change, what must not regress, intentional traits to preserve, real defects still to reject and non-use/precedence cases. Design a realistic showcase with its exact prompt and a distinct additional brief or fixture that tests reuse beyond that showcase.

**Output:** domain-native production profile, source-to-behaviour-to-test mapping, showcase design, evaluation cases and implementation plan.

**Exit:** guidance is operational and bounded; evaluation can distinguish useful specialisation from metadata, prompt detail or a one-off example.

### P6 — Implement and demonstrate

**Inputs:** accepted profile and evaluation design, available core implementation and required execution capabilities.

Use the existing domain pack-authoring skill where available, extending it only as required. Keep command names domain-owned; do not add a generic research command hierarchy merely to mirror these stages.

Implement a self-contained pack with concise relevant guidance and source provenance. Include necessary runtime guidance locally rather than depending on another pack, research logs or original books. Reuse research during development without introducing package inheritance or runtime coupling.

Create the showcase README with the exact copyable prompt, run the showcase and preserve the actual artefacts and execution provenance. Create the behavioural fixtures and run available local checks. Keep provider execution in existing tools; preserve applicable voice, licence, security and publication safeguards. A prompt alone is not a demonstration.

**Output:** installable pack, local references/evals, showcase prompt and generated artefacts, execution record and validation results.

**Exit:** the intended production path has actually run, the output is inspectable and the package is implemented without hidden repository-relative dependencies. Unavailable execution is recorded as a blocker, not a successful showcase.

### P7 — Evaluate, validate installation and catalogue

**Inputs:** implemented pack, core baseline, showcase and independent reuse case, predefined evaluation criteria.

Compare core-only and core-plus-pack runs on the same substantive brief, constraints and comparable execution conditions. Record core/pack revisions, prompts, model/tool versions where relevant, settings, resource limits and deviations. The intentional pack difference is activation and its packaged guidance; do not give the packed run a richer task brief. Judge the production artefacts and decisions, not whether they mention the pack's name.

Where useful, compare against core plus ordinary project-specific instructions to determine whether packaging adds reusable depth, consistency or reduced prompting burden. Preserve unsuccessful and inconclusive results. Account for stochastic variation and evaluator limitations; do not infer general superiority from one favourable sample or force all quality dimensions into one score.

Test activation and non-activation, relevant specialised behaviour, precedence, preservation, pack-aware evaluation without hiding defects, domain boundaries, negative/incompatibility cases and targeted refinement. Use the additional brief/fixture to check that the pack is not just a recipe for the showcase premise.

Run the owning repository's checks and a clean consumer-project installation/use smoke test. Local checks and external installation are separate gates. Report observed outcomes and unresolved limitations. If useful specialised behaviour is not demonstrated or regressions are unacceptable, refine and retest, or retain the entry as unproven/deferred; do not publish it as ready.

**Output:** comparative production evidence, behavioural results, installation evidence, limitations, readiness decision and catalogue update.

**Exit:** a ready-to-use claim is supported by actual production, evaluation and clean-installation evidence. Planned or unproven entries remain explicitly labelled and do not imply a project maturity promotion.

## 5. Evidence reuse and source roles

A five-book corpus may combine craft/professional knowledge, creative exemplars and critical/analytical work where appropriate. These are possible roles, not compulsory slots. Each source must earn its place through its contribution to the specialisation.

Craft books supply candidate methods and limitations. Creative exemplars support observations about techniques actually present in the examined work, not claims that those techniques caused success or apply universally. Critical works offer comparisons and interpretations; they do not establish direct examination of a work they discuss. Distinguish source observation, researcher interpretation and production hypothesis.

Never turn exemplars into a request to reproduce an existing work's plot, characters or expression, or make an individual creator the complete pack definition. Carry over the domain's existing creative and voice safeguards.

Share source records and reviewed extraction references where useful, with stable locations/revisions and pack-specific applicability notes. Do not copy complete research logs into every pack. Changes to a shared finding require a bounded impact review of consuming guidance and tests; retain unaffected work and identify stale evidence rather than silently treating it as current.

The [domain research process](domain-research-process.md) continues to govern permissions, access and publication. Reuse does not waive those rules, multiply independent corroboration, or make books runtime dependencies.

## 6. Catalogue and evidence surfaces

Specification 05 owns the contract; specification 06 owns the curated selection. Keep detailed research in the owning domain's `docs/research-logs/`, with one substantive record per stage and supporting files only where size or reuse warrants them. Existing records may satisfy a stage after review; do not add a database or a universal metadata schema.

Each catalogue entry should identify:

```text
identity, intended use and non-use
selection rationale and complementary catalogue contribution
core baseline and specialised behavioural difference
five-book corpus and each book's contribution
research records, source access/reading limits and unresolved questions
hard constraints, soft defaults and qualified methods
relevant core-skill effects and preserved boundaries
showcase, exact prompt and actual outputs when available
specific acceptance cases and comparative evaluation evidence
implementation, research and evaluation status separately
installation evidence, limitations and compatibility/migration notes
```

Use domain-owned status terms with clear meanings. For example, research may be planned/partial/complete, implementation planned/implemented, and evaluation not-run/passed/failed/inconclusive. Record readiness separately. Do not equate a listed entry, completed bibliography, implemented directory or prompt with demonstrated quality.

## 7. New-project integration and existing-project migration

During a new project bootstrap, use catalogue curation and P1–P5 to design the pack architecture and evidence-qualified catalogue. Schedule P2, P3 and P4 as explicit, separately committed per-pack research stages; do not compress them into the architecture-stage summary. P6–P7 follow when the core vertical and required execution paths are available. The catalogue may retain additional planned candidates, clearly separated from researched specifications and implemented packs.

For established projects, enter through the existing domain repository and authoring skill. Reuse accepted core contracts and adequate research rather than rerunning the full project bootstrap. A new pack follows this process; an existing pack or catalogue adopts it through an explicit migration decision preserving approved work, identities, interfaces, stage history and evidence.

The [Narrative catalogue](https://github.com/sb-dev/narrative-production-skills/blob/feat/extension-packs-and-benchmark/docs/06-extension-pack-catalogue.md) is a reference application, not a fixed catalogue or reading list. Its future regeneration should align the domain's pack contract, catalogue, authoring skill and evaluation together. This family process does not itself change that repository, perform its book research, regenerate its catalogue or certify its packs.

The [family decision log](../research-logs/2026-09-10-extension-pack-bootstrap-research-foundation.md) records the adoption rationale, review scenarios and migration boundary.
