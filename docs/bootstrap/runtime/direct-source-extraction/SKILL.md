# Direct Source Extraction

The governing bootstrap defines corpus size, selection/substitution permissions, reading requirements and exit criteria. This skill supplies source-handling mechanics, not a second research method.

## Discover and identify

Inspect accessible task attachments and local `books/` recursively, including `.PDF` filenames. Ignored files may require explicit local enumeration rather than Git-aware search. Never invent mounted paths or claim a filename, preview or abstract provides full text.

Read document metadata/title pages where available. Match title, author and edition/version against accepted source records; do not rely on filename similarity. Recognise duplicate copies of the same work rather than counting PDFs as distinct foundational books. Record ambiguous identity rather than guessing.

Use a stable source label and bibliographic metadata in durable logs. A non-sensitive repository-relative identifier is acceptable for local material; keep private absolute paths, upload tokens and transient attachment locators out of committed output.

## Corpus and access

- Before selection is accepted, include all supplied sources in the governing supplied-book decision process. More than the required corpus size needs the specified exclusion/demotion approval; fewer books permit complementary selection only under the governing rules.
- No local books is not an automatic Stage 1 blocker. Identify access needs at selection; required direct examination cannot pass without adequate source access.
- After selection is accepted, preserve it. Inventory newly supplied material without silently substituting or reopening the corpus; apply the governing process or obtain the required decision.
- Reuse adequate persisted direct-source evidence after reviewing edition, reading scope and applicability. A source need not be reacquired merely to resume a stage that does not require new reading.
- If new direct reading is required and a source is inaccessible, identify the exact book/edition and missing access. Attachments may be session-scoped; do not claim they will survive a fresh cloud session.

For each source record identity, supplied/selected origin, access state, intended contribution, material actually examined and limitations. Keep `available`, `examined`, `selected` and `accepted` distinct.

## Examine and reconcile

Read enough contiguous source material to understand the relevant argument, conditions and counterexamples. Famous passages, search hits or publisher summaries alone do not constitute meaningful examination.

For each material finding preserve:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Provenance | Source/edition and chapter, section, page or stable location actually examined |
| Finding | Independently expressed principle, method, claim or heuristic |
| Applicability | Assumptions, prerequisites, scope and limitations |
| Production effect | Decisions, workflow, artefacts or responsibilities affected |
| Evaluation and repair | Evidence/checks, failure conditions and correction implications |
| Relationships | Support, overlap, tension or contradiction with other sources |
| Disposition | Retain, merge, adapt, qualify, reject or investigate further |

Reconcile overlap and conflicts; repeated claims are not automatically independent corroboration. Do not force every book into a core rule. Hand unresolved claims and missing coverage to broader challenge research.

## Privacy and completion

Never commit, rename, move, overwrite or delete supplied PDFs. Keep optional local extraction caches inside ignored `books/`; do not publish source text or reconstructed chapters. Use local reading/parsing first. Uploading a book as a task attachment does not authorise forwarding it to Firecrawl or another service.

Completion requires meaningful examination of every required source for its intended contribution, honest coverage, traceable findings, explicit limitations/conflicts and the governing stage's exit criteria. A bibliography or model memory cannot replace missing examination.
